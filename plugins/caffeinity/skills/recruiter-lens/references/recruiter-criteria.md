# Recruiter Evaluation Criteria

## What Recruiters Actually Evaluate

### The 6-Second Scan Priorities

Recruiters commonly report spending only a few seconds on initial resume screening (studies suggest 6-8 seconds on average). In this time, they look for:

1. **Current role and company** - Are they at a relevant/recognizable organization?
2. **Job title trajectory** - Is there logical career progression?
3. **Education/certifications** - Do they meet minimum qualifications?
4. **Employment timeline** - Any obvious gaps or job-hopping?
5. **Contact information** - Can we reach them easily?
6. **Overall presentation** - Does it look professional and organized?

### Deep Review Criteria (Post-Scan)

If the resume passes the initial scan, recruiters evaluate (the weights are typical relative emphasis, not measured data):

#### 1. Role Fit (40% weight)
- Direct experience with required tools/technologies
- Industry relevance
- Seniority level alignment
- Scope of previous responsibilities

#### 2. Evidence of Impact (30% weight)
- Quantified achievements vs. duty lists
- Complexity of problems solved
- Scale of projects managed
- Measurable business outcomes

#### 3. Career Trajectory (15% weight)
- Progressive responsibility increases
- Logical transitions between roles
- Skill development over time
- Stability vs. job-hopping

#### 4. Risk Factors (15% weight)
- Unexplained employment gaps
- Frequent job changes without context
- Title inconsistencies
- Overqualification or underqualification signals

## Common Rejection Reasons (Data-Backed)

### Immediate Rejections (No Human Review)

1. **ATS parse failure** - Resume format breaks automated extraction
2. **Missing contact info** - Can't reach the candidate
3. **File corruption** - Can't open the document
4. **Wrong file type** - .pages, .docx when PDF requested

### Quick Rejections (<30 seconds)

1. **Obvious job-hopping** - 4+ jobs in 2 years without explanation
2. **Unexplained gap** - 12+ months with no activity listed
3. **Generic spray-and-pray** - Clearly mass-applied with no tailoring
4. **Poor presentation** - Typos, inconsistent formatting, unprofessional elements
5. **Title mismatch** - Applying for senior role with junior-level experience

### Considered Rejections (After Full Review)

1. **Missing must-haves** - Lacks critical skills/experience from JD
2. **Weak impact statements** - Only lists responsibilities, no outcomes
3. **Overqualification** - Too senior, seen as flight risk
4. **Industry pivot without bridge** - No transferable skills highlighted
5. **Salary mismatch** - Expectations far above budget (if disclosed)

## Positive Signals Recruiters Look For

### Strong Indicators

- **Quantified achievements** - "Increased revenue 25%" not "Responsible for sales"
- **Recognizable companies** - Known brands provide credibility anchors
- **Clear progression** - Promotions or increasing scope over time
- **Relevant certifications** - Industry-recognized credentials present
- **Tailored content** - Resume clearly matches the specific role
- **Professional presentation** - Clean, consistent, easy to scan
- **Specific technical skills** - Named tools/platforms matching JD

### Moderate Indicators

- **Education from known institutions** - Provides baseline credibility
- **Volunteer/work side projects** - Shows initiative and skill application
- **Publications/patents** - Demonstrates expertise depth
- **Speaking engagements** - Industry recognition
- **Open source contributions** - Technical credibility (for tech roles)

## Red Flags That Raise Concerns

### Critical Red Flags

- **Employment gaps >6 months** without explanation
- **Job-hopping pattern** - 3+ roles in <2 years
- **Title inflation** - "VP" at unknown 2-person startup
- **Vague company descriptions** - Can't verify legitimacy
- **Inconsistent dates** - Suggests fabrication
- **Typos in first paragraph** - Lack of attention to detail

### Moderate Red Flags

- **Generic objective statement** - "Seeking challenging opportunity"
- **All responsibility, no impact** - Lists duties but not outcomes
- **Outdated skills** - Technologies no longer in use
- **Age-revealing details** - Graduation year >15 years ago (can trigger bias)
- **Overly long resume** - >2 pages for <10 years experience
- **Unprofessional email** - partydude99@...

### Minor Red Flags

- **Missing LinkedIn URL** - Harder to verify background
- **No summary section** - Makes quick assessment harder
- **Inconsistent bullet formatting** - Looks sloppy
- **Irrelevant hobbies** - Takes up valuable space
- **"References available"** - Wastes space on outdated convention

## Interview Decision Framework

### Automatic Interview Invite

- All must-haves met with strong evidence
- Quantified impact in relevant areas
- Clear career progression
- No significant red flags
- Resume tailored to role

### Maybe Pile (Phone Screen First)

- Most must-haves met, some partial
- Some impact evidence but incomplete
- Minor red flags that need clarification
- Good potential but needs verification

### Automatic Reject

- Missing critical must-haves
- No quantified achievements
- Multiple red flags
- Generic, untailored resume
- Poor presentation/formatting

## Industry-Specific Variations

### Tech Roles

**Prioritize:**
- Specific technologies and frameworks
- GitHub/portfolio links
- Open source contributions
- System design experience
- Scale handled (users, data volume, transactions)

**Red Flags:**
- Vague technical claims ("proficient in many languages")
- No code samples or projects
- Outdated tech stack only

### Finance/Consulting

**Prioritize:**
- Prestige of previous employers/clients
- Deal size or project scope
- Certifications (CFA, CPA, etc.)
- Educational pedigree
- Quantifiable business impact

**Red Flags:**
- Unknown companies without context
- No deal/project specifics
- Missing standard certifications for level

### Creative/Marketing

**Prioritize:**
- Portfolio quality and relevance
- Campaign results/metrics
- Brand names worked with
- Creative tools proficiency
- Awards/recognition

**Red Flags:**
- No portfolio link
- Vague campaign descriptions
- No measurable results
- Outdated design trends in portfolio

### Healthcare

**Prioritize:**
- Required licenses/certifications
- Specialization alignment
- Patient population experience
- Facility types worked in
- Continuing education

**Red Flags:**
- Missing required licenses
- Gaps in clinical experience
- No recent continuing education

## Compensation Alignment Signals

### Overqualified Indicators

- Current title significantly higher than target role
- Years of experience 2x+ the requirement
- Previous compensation disclosed well above budget
- Managing teams when applying for individual contributor role

**Risk:** Candidate will leave quickly for better opportunity

### Underqualified Indicators

- Missing core technical requirements
- Significantly fewer years than specified
- No experience with required scale/complexity
- Junior titles when applying for senior role

**Risk:** Won't be able to perform at required level

## Remote Work Assessment

### Remote-Ready Signals

- Previous remote/hybrid experience noted
- Self-management examples in achievements
- Communication tools proficiency listed
- Distributed team collaboration mentioned
- Time zone flexibility indicated

### Remote Risk Signals

- No remote experience
- Highly collaborative work style emphasized
- No mention of async communication skills
- Location constraints unclear

## Diversity & Inclusion Considerations

### Bias Triggers to Avoid

Recruiters trained in D&I watch for unconscious bias:

- Age-revealing information (graduation years, early career details)
- Gender-coded language in self-descriptions
- Cultural assumptions in hobby/interest sections
- Socioeconomic signals from education/activity descriptions

### Best Practices for Candidates

- Remove graduation year if >10 years ago (unless recently graduated)
- Use gender-neutral achievement language
- Focus on professional activities, not personal circumstances
- Highlight diverse team collaboration experience
