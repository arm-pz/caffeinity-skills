# Awareness map

The buyer's awareness level decides what the first line may claim, what has to be taught, which
proof lands and what the CTA can reasonably ask for. Get this wrong and well-built copy still
fails, so pick a level and write it in the header of every asset.

## The five levels

The scale below is the common direct-response framing. Levels are a useful working model, not a
measurement; a real audience contains all five at once, so name the dominant one for each asset and
each traffic source separately.

### 1. Unaware

They live with the problem but have not named it. Nothing in their feed points at it.

- **Message**: a story, an observation or a pattern that makes the problem visible. No product, no
  price, no jargon.
- **Headline angle**: curiosity or identity. A claim about the world that makes them check their own
  experience. Never a benefit the reader has not asked for yet.
- **Lead**: narrative or a demonstration. Earn attention before making a request.
- **Proof that carries**: a demonstration they can watch, an authority the reader already trusts,
  a result expressed as a story.
- **CTA**: a low-cost ask. Read, watch, take the quiz. Asking for a purchase from unaware traffic
  burns the budget.
- **Assets that fit**: the ad, the video, the lead magnet, the first emails of a sequence.

### 2. Problem-aware

They know the problem and feel it. They do not know a solution exists in particular.

- **Message**: name the problem more precisely than they can, then introduce the category of
  solution that fixes it. The value here is being understood, then informed.
- **Headline angle**: pain, or the cost of the pain. "Still [problem]" and the consequence of
  another quarter of it.
- **Lead**: agitate honestly, then explain why the problem persists; the mechanism carries the page
  from here.
- **Proof that carries**: shared experience ("people in your situation"), then numbers once the
  mechanism is on the table.
- **CTA**: something that solves the problem without a big commitment. A guide, a diagnostic, a
  call.
- **Assets that fit**: cold social ads, the sales page's problem section, top-of-funnel emails.

### 3. Solution-aware

They know solutions like this exist and are comparing types. They have not chosen you.

- **Message**: differentiate the mechanism. Why this class of solution works and why this
  implementation is built differently.
- **Headline angle**: the different method, or the failure of the usual route. Comparison framing
  starts to work.
- **Lead**: direct claim plus the mechanism, then the failed-alternatives section.
- **Proof that carries**: comparisons, demonstrations, specific results with a timeframe, the
  objection-killer testimonial.
- **CTA**: trial, demo, comparison, or the purchase if the price is small.
- **Assets that fit**: search ads, most sales pages, product descriptions, mid-sequence emails.

### 4. Product-aware

They know the product. They have not decided, usually on price, timing, trust or fit.

- **Message**: sharpen the offer. What is included, terms, guarantee, the reason to act now, and the
  one objection doing the blocking.
- **Headline angle**: the new angle on a known product: bonus, deal, proof they had not seen, or the
  objection answered in the headline.
- **Lead**: offer first, then the reason it is worth it.
- **Proof that carries**: named customer results from their own segment, the guarantee, support
  responsiveness.
- **CTA**: buy, book, start. Say what happens in the ninety seconds after the click.
- **Assets that fit**: retargeting ads, launch emails, checkout and cart-recovery flows, upsell
  pages.

### 5. Most aware

They want it and are ready; only the deal or a last doubt remains.

- **Message**: terms, urgency that is true, and an instruction. Keep the copy short.
- **Headline angle**: the number, the deadline, or the incentive. Nothing clever.
- **Lead**: state the deal.
- **Proof that carries**: almost none. Risk reduction (terms, refund, cancellation) outperforms
  persuasion at this level.
- **CTA**: the button, plus what happens next.
- **Assets that fit**: last-call emails, checkout page, deadline banner, the final VSL close.

## Quick map

| Level | What the headline may claim | First section of body | Ask | Typical asset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | Something about their world | Story or demonstration | Attention | Ad, video |
| Problem-aware | The pain, precisely | Agitation, then cause | Low-cost lead | Cold ad, sales page lead |
| Solution-aware | Why this method differs | Failed alternatives, mechanism | Demo or trial | Search ad, sales page |
| Product-aware | The offer or the answer to their doubt | Offer build, terms | Purchase | Retargeting, launch email |
| Most aware | The deal | The instruction | Purchase now | Last-call email, checkout |

## Choosing the level when nobody said

- Paid search to a product page: solution-aware unless the query set says otherwise.
- Paid social to a sales page: problem-aware to solution-aware. Assume problem-aware and test.
- Retargeting and abandoned cart: product-aware, and the copy should say so in the header.
- A launch to an owned list that has heard of you: product-aware for the reveal, with a
  problem-aware warm-up email ahead of it.
- Webinar attendees: solution-aware, since they registered for the topic.

If the user cannot say, write the asset at one level, print `Awareness assumed: [level]` in the
header, and add a second headline set written at the adjacent level so the test is ready.

## Across a sequence

Awareness escalates, so the message must move with it. An email sequence that pitches at level five
in email one and a sales page that opens at level one are the same mistake in opposite directions.
For a launch: open at where the list actually is, teach or agitate while it is still relevant, then
move to offer, objection and terms as the level rises. Cart recovery sits at product-aware and
should not reopen the problem; the buyer already decided, so answer the doubt and reduce the risk.

## Proof, by level

The testimonial framework in `references/assets.md` supplies the questions and the blank structure;
level decides which shape goes where. Unaware and problem-aware readers respond to shared
experience and demonstration. Solution-aware readers usually weigh specific results with a
timeframe and a comparison. Product-aware and most-aware readers usually weigh risk reduction: terms,
guarantee, support. None of these justifies inventing the underlying quote, number or name.

## Temperature, traffic source and the mismatch to watch

Awareness is about what the buyer knows; temperature is about how willing they are to act. A
solution-aware but cold audience still needs the mechanism taught before the offer. Three
mismatches worth checking first:

1. **Ad promises the outcome, page opens with the story.** The click arrives expecting an answer and
   gets a warm-up. Repeat the ad's promise in the page's first line.
2. **Warm list, cold-traffic copy.** A list that knows you does not need a long problem agitation;
   it needs the offer and the objection handled.
3. **Unaware traffic, direct-response close.** Asking a purchase from a stranger in one page. Route
   through a lower-cost action instead and say which one.
