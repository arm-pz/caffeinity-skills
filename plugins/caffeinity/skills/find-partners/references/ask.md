# Writing the collaboration ask

Read this before step 4. One file per partner: `outreach/asks/<slug>.md`.

## Where links fit

Links appear in collaborations because they help readers: a guest post cites a source, an
integration page links the product it integrates with, an interview links the guest. That's
editorial linking, and it's fine. Google's spam policies call it a link scheme when the link is the
deal itself: "I link to you, you link to me", three-way swaps, or links paid for without
`rel="sponsored"`. So:

- **Never offer or ask for a link swap**, direct or three-way, and never make a link a condition of
  anything.
- **Never pay for a followed link.** If the user chooses a paid placement (a newsletter sponsorship,
  a sponsored post), the ask says it's sponsored and that the link will carry `rel="sponsored"`.
- **Value goes both ways in the content, not in links.** The partner gets an article, data, an
  expert, an audience or an integration. The user gets exposure to the partner's audience.

If the user asks for an exchange anyway, explain this once in a sentence, offer the closest
collaboration from the table below, and go on. Don't build a swap list.

## Pick the collaboration

| Collaboration | Choose when | What the user must supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guest post on their site | they publish contributed posts | 3 headline ideas tied to their audience, a writer |
| Expert quote / roundup | they write roundups or need sources | a named expert and 2–3 sentences of real insight |
| Co-made content (data study, guide, template) | both have data or expertise the other lacks | the user's half of the data or work |
| Integration or partner listing | the products connect, or could | a working integration, or a plan for one |
| Podcast or webinar | they host one | a speaker and a topic |
| Newsletter feature | they run a newsletter with a features slot | a useful resource, not an ad (or a disclosed sponsorship) |

## When they refuse promotion

Good publishers say "no promotional pieces for your product". Pitch the experience instead: a field
report, a comparison, a failure and its fix. The product appears once, where it's the honest
source of the example. If the piece only works as an ad, it's the wrong piece for that
publisher.

## Shape

```
To: <contact_route>            (source: <contact_source>)
Subject: <collaboration, specific>: e.g. "Guest post idea for <site>: <headline>"

Hi <first name>,

<One sentence showing you know their work: a recent post, an episode, an integration.>

<The proposal: what you'd make or give, for which of their readers, by when.>

<Why you: one fact from the brief's Proof line, or the expert's credential.>

<The easy next step: "Want me to send an outline?" / "Happy to draft it for your review.">

<Signer name>, <role>
```

Under 120 words. One ask per partner, for one collaboration.
