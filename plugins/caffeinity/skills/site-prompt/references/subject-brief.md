# Subject brief and swap test

The brief is the difference between a subject-tailored site and a template with the nouns changed. Fill it before naming colours, sections or motion.

## Brief template

| Field | What goes in it | Bad | Good (cargo ship) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | One concrete instance, not a category | "shipping" | A fictional container liner on one Asia–Europe rotation |
| Audience | Who the page serves and what they already know | "everyone" | Freight forwarders booking 40ft containers |
| Job | The one thing the page must let them do | "learn about us" | Get a quote for a named sailing, or follow one container |
| Vocabulary | At least eight terms a practitioner uses | "cargo" | TEU, deadweight, draft, berth, port rotation, bill of lading, manifest, demurrage, transshipment, cut-off, stevedore |
| Artifacts | The forms and objects of the world | "photos" | Manifest, bill of lading, port rotation, stowage plan, tide table, AIS track |
| Signature mechanic | One interaction only this subject earns | "scroll animations" | The route inks port by port; the selected container advances and the cut-off counts down |
| Tension | What the page dramatizes or argues | "we are the best" | Most cargo is invisible; the page makes one box legible |
| Honest claims | What can be said, and what must be labelled | "99.9% on time" | Fictional line; schedule illustrative; no performance metrics |

## Worked example: SELENE (lunar expedition)

- **Subject:** a concept lunar expedition operator running six to twenty-eight day trips.
- **Audience:** affluent experience-seekers.
- **Job:** put a name on the manifest.
- **Vocabulary:** translunar injection, lunar orbit insertion, regolith, Mare Tranquillitatis, mission clock, launch window.
- **Artifacts:** mission log, trajectory, mission classes, launch-window countdown, manifest form.
- **Signature mechanic:** a scroll-driven Earthrise cut into six photographic layers, then an Earth-to-Moon route that inks itself leg by leg.
- **Tension:** the first holiday that leaves the planet.
- **Sections earned:** Earthrise, mission log, trajectory, mission classes, reserve. Every one has a subject reason.
- **Sections not earned:** SaaS pricing tiers, a customer-logo wall, a blog grid. They would survive a topic swap.

## Worked example: cargo ship (container liner)

- **Subject:** a fictional container line on one Asia–Europe rotation, for example Shanghai, Singapore, Suez, Rotterdam, Felixstowe.
- **Audience:** freight forwarders and small shippers comparing sailings.
- **Job:** request a quote for a named sailing, or follow one container.
- **Vocabulary:** TEU, deadweight, draft, berth, port rotation, bill of lading, manifest, demurrage, transshipment, cut-off, tide window, stevedore.
- **Visual world:** marine chart lines and soundings, container livery, oxidized steel, stencil lettering, port timestamps, tide tables. Palette: deep sea black, signal orange, salt white, rust. Type: condensed industrial sans for display, humanist sans for text, mono for the port log.
- **Signature mechanic:** the page follows one container from berth to berth. A chart-like route inks leg by leg; the current port and cut-off countdown are functions of the same scroll progress. Alternative: an inspectable stowage plan. Pick one, not both.
- **Tension:** the network that moves most of what we own is invisible; the page makes one box legible.
- **Sections earned:** a hero naming one sailing; the port rotation; the manifest of one container; the vessel (capacity, draft, berths); a quote form tied to a cut-off.
- **Sections not earned:** a "features" card grid, a "trusted by" logo wall, generic testimonials. They are not of the subject.

### Contrast

| Question | SELENE | Cargo ship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | The Earth rising over the Moon | A named sailing leaving a berth, or a route chart |
| Motion | Dust clears, the Earth climbs | The route inks, the container advances, the cut-off ticks |
| Proof | Mission classes and a drawn trajectory | The rotation, vessel specs and a manifest |
| Form | Add me to the manifest | Quote against a sailing cut-off |
| Carry-over | Its own scroll loop | None of SELENE's mechanics |

## Swap test

1. Replace the subject with something unrelated, for example a coffee subscription.
2. Keep every section, transition and line of copy.
3. If more than a little survives unchanged, the concept is templated. Cut or redesign the parts that survive.
4. Read the vocabulary: would a practitioner recognize their world, or only the nouns?
5. Test the signature mechanic: could it belong to another subject? If yes, it is not a signature.
6. Test the hero: if the headline could be swapped with another industry's, it says nothing.

Run the swap test twice: once after the brief and once after the build. The second run catches drift introduced by implementation.

## Template smells

- Hero = eyebrow + large headline + subhead + two CTAs + gradient.
- Three feature cards with generic icons.
- A "trusted by" wall of invented or unrelated logos.
- Scroll reveals on every section, regardless of meaning.
- Stock photography standing in for the subject.
- Copy that survives any industry: empower, seamless, next-gen, revolutionize, unlock.
- Invented metrics, testimonials, certifications or ratings.
- Reusing the previous site's mechanics because they worked once.

## One-word topics

Treat a single word as a category, not a subject.

- **ship** → for a commercial B2B page, narrow to a container liner or shipbroker; for consumer travel, a ferry route. State the choice and why in the prompt's brief.
- **coffee** → a single roaster, a farm, a subscription, or a cafe. Choose one, with its own ritual and artifacts.
- **fitness** → a coaching practice, a gym, an app. Each earns different sections.

If the user later names a different concrete subject ("ferry", not "container liner"), redesign the concept. A find-and-replace is a failed pass.

## Copy rules

- Write final copy, no lorem ipsum and no bracketed placeholders.
- Use specific nouns and named places; label illustrative dates, prices and schedules.
- Match the subject's voice: a manifest reads like a manifest, a charter quote like a quote.
- Use at least eight subject terms naturally. Do not stuff keywords into headings.
- Make no claim the operator could not defend. When in doubt, mark it as a concept.
